v0.4 distribution-aware geometry experiment¶

Equations and gradient path¶

The transformer predicts A = diag(d) + U U^T, where d is produced by a softplus plus 1e-4. Overdamped training uses

\[z_{n+1}=z_n-hA\nabla U(z_n)+\sqrt{2Th}\,(D^{1/2}\xi+U\eta),\]

with the bounded transformed potential U(z) = f(x(z)) - T log|dx/dz|. BAOAB uses the same factor as a mass matrix, its Woodbury inverse in the drift, and exact Ornstein--Uhlenbeck momentum covariance T(1-exp(-2 gamma h)) M. Explicit PRNG keys make trajectories reproducible and pathwise differentiation carries gradients through both drift geometry and noise scale. The BAOAB rollout also samples its initial momentum from N(0, T M) using the first predicted mass, matching the inference transition rather than beginning from an artificial zero-momentum state.

The weak Fokker--Planck term compares the conditional expected one-step change of phi(z)=mean(z^2) with

\[L\phi=b^T\nabla\phi+\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(a\nabla^2\phi), \qquad a=2TA.\]

Mobility is frozen only within an episode. Updating it from trajectory history makes the optimizer nonstationary. Euler and BAOAB retain finite-step bias; the experiment makes no exact Gibbs or finite-step stationarity claim. The quadratic Gaussian noise contribution is evaluated analytically rather than sampled, avoiding a variance term that would reward shrinking mobility.

Protocol¶

  • Model: width 16, four heads, rank two, one transformer layer.
  • Training: 36 Adam updates at 3e-4, global gradient clipping, seed 17.
  • Trajectory: six episodes, two integration steps per episode, horizons 2/4/6, step size 0.008, temperature 0.5.
  • Training set: shifted, rotated, condition-10 to condition-1000 quadratics, Rastrigin, Ackley, and Rosenbrock in dimensions two through four.
  • Held out: ten paired instances spanning rotated and ill-conditioned quadratics, Rosenbrock, Ackley, and Rastrigin in unseen dimensions three to six and seeds 40/41.
  • Primary evaluation: objective budget 80 and temperature 0.5. Paired percentile bootstrap intervals use 10,000 resamples.
  • Transfer evaluation: temperatures 0.25/1.0 and budgets 40/120 on four held-out instances. These small four-instance intervals are descriptive.

The machine-readable record is results/geometry-training-v0.4.0.json. The three portable checkpoints are in artifacts/neuralopt-v0.4.0-*.npz. Reproduce with uv run python scripts/run_geometry_training_v0_4.py; use --reuse-checkpoints to repeat evaluation without retraining.

The direct paired FP-minus-regret comparison is -0.048 with 95% interval [-0.105, -0.006] for overdamped dynamics (5/10 wins). This is evidence on the small prespecified held-out set, not a universal advantage. For BAOAB transfer the difference is -0.220 [-0.570, 0.022], so no improvement is established.

At unseen temperatures and budgets the overdamped FP-minus-regret point estimate remained negative in all four settings, but every four-instance interval touched or crossed zero. At budget 40, transferred FP geometry was worse for BAOAB at both temperatures (+0.377 at 0.25 and +0.357 at 1.0). The simple fixed diagonal remains the strongest supported BAOAB result.

Training took 22.9 seconds for overdamped regret, 28.9 seconds for BAOAB regret, and 17.3 seconds for regret plus FP on the recorded CPU run. Median warm inference was about 0.08--0.12 seconds. First-call timings in the shared process are cache- and order-sensitive and are retained in the artifact rather than interpreted as isolated compilation measurements.

Correctness and remaining gaps¶

Tests verify positive diagonals, diagonal-plus-low-rank covariance, exact OU fluctuation--dissipation scaling, reproducibility, Gaussian moments, finite nonzero gradients into both geometry heads, weak generator identities, and nonfinite-coordinate rejection. Certification code and independent replay are unchanged.

The experiment is still small: six training tasks, ten primary held-out pairs, one training seed, and no BBOB or scientific inverse problem. FP weighting was not selected on a separate validation sweep, free-energy regularization remains disabled, and objective closures compile independently. Stronger claims require multiple training seeds, larger held-out suites, family-level uncertainty, and batched parameterized task generators.
