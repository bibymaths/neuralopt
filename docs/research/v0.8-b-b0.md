## Deviations and caveats

1. **The BB tuning grid is smaller than the plan's.** The plan listed an initial step of
   {1e-3, 1e-2, 1e-1} and a step clip of {1, 10}. NeuralOpt's compiled BB loop (the Gram
   loop with zero parameters, which v0.7 checked equals BB to 1e-10) has no step-clip
   parameter. So only the fallback (initial) length was tuned.
2. **Owned L-BFGS traces:** rejected line-search trials count in the units, but the solver
   does not expose their values. Such a trial could be lower than the current value only
   by the tiny Armijo margin (c1 = 1e-4), so the arm's anytime regret is very slightly
   pessimistic.
3. **CMA-ES** evaluates the shared start point first (1 unit), then runs its generations.
   The last generation is cut at the budget and not told to the optimizer.
4. **The B0 rule's "best baseline" is the maximum over all arms and configurations** on
   the same 200 instances. That is optimistic for baselines, so it makes "no headroom"
   easier to declare.
   - For Rosenbrock and Family 2b, the conclusion does not depend on it: several arms
     exceed 0.95, and the zero-search polish alone reaches 0.88 and 0.98.
5. **The basin metric saturates where the polish does the work.** The uncharged
   2,000-unit polish defines the basin operationally (plan §5.2). On Family 2b and
   Rosenbrock, almost every start lies in the global basin, so C12 cannot separate arms
   there. The B0 rule then also drops them from C11, as the plan specifies. Their anytime
   regret still differs between arms (owned multistart −3.9 versus BB −2.9 on Family 2b),
   but that is not tested.
6. **Rastrigin and Ackley may give little signal on C12.** Basin rates of 0–17% for every
   baseline mean a policy must find the global basin among thousands of local minima
   within 100 evaluations. A negative or inconclusive C12 there is a plausible outcome.
