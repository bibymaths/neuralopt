Hamiltonian related work¶

This review was checked against primary papers and author/reference-manual sources available through 15 November 2026.

Duane et al. (1987) introduced hybrid Monte Carlo; Neal's 2011 handbook chapter established the modern practical account. Hoffman and Gelman's NUTS removes a fixed trajectory-length choice and uses diminishing warm-up adaptation. It is not implemented here because the first experiment isolates fixed integration length and learned mass geometry.

Girolami and Calderhead (2011, JRSS B) introduced Riemannian-manifold HMC, whose position-dependent metric requires nonseparable dynamics, log-determinant terms, and generalized integration. NeuralOpt does not claim RMHMC: its metric is fixed inside each transition. Betancourt's review explains why matching the Euclidean metric to target covariance can improve exploration, but this does not imply that an optimizer-trained preconditioner is automatically a good HMC mass.

Pakman and Paninski (2014, JCGS) developed exact HMC for truncated Gaussian models; Afshar and Domke (2015, NeurIPS) studied reflection/refraction HMC. Those methods motivate correct boundary dynamics. The present implementation instead uses a logistic change of variables and includes its Jacobian, avoiding claims that clipping is reversible or volume preserving.

Levy, Hoffman, and Sohl-Dickstein (2018, ICLR), “Generalizing Hamiltonian Monte Carlo with Neural Networks,” introduced L2HMC, learning reversible volume-preserving transformations. Hoffman et al. (2019), “NeuTra-lizing Bad Geometry in Hamiltonian Monte Carlo,” learned transport maps before HMC. These works learn proposal geometry while preserving explicit sampling corrections. NeuralOpt's history-adaptive transformer metric is more modest and is treated as an optimization heuristic outside the fixed-kernel reference experiment.

Temperature schedules relate the artificial density to simulated annealing (Kirkpatrick, Gelatt, and Vecchi, 1983). Changing beta changes the target; an annealed optimization trajectory is not a sample from one stationary density and does not by itself prove global convergence. Temperature learning remains unimplemented pending evidence that fixed-temperature Hamiltonian candidates justify the added complexity.
