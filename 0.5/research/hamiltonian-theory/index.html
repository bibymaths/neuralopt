Hamiltonian-informed optimization¶

Mathematical facts¶

For fixed inverse temperature beta and mass matrix M, NeuralOpt defines the artificial density pi(x) proportional to exp(-beta f(x)) and Hamiltonian

\[H(x,p)=\beta f(x)+\tfrac12p^T M^{-1}p,\qquad p\sim N(0,M).\]

The implementation uses velocity M^-1 p, not M p. Its learned convention is explicit: the transformer output D + U U^T is the mass matrix. Momentum is sampled as sqrt(D) z + U eta, and Woodbury algebra applies the inverse without forming a dense matrix.

Leapfrog alternates a half momentum update, full position update, and half momentum update. For a fixed separable Hamiltonian it is reversible (with final momentum reversal), symplectic, and volume preserving. The Metropolis decision is evaluated in log space: log(u) < min(0, H_initial - H_proposed). These are the standard constructions described by the Oxford and Washington lecture notes, Hoffman and Gelman, Betancourt, and the Stan reference manual.

Box constraints use x = lo + (hi-lo) sigmoid(z). The unconstrained potential is

\[U(z)=\beta f(x(z))-\log|dx/dz|.\]

The Jacobian term is required for the transformed target. Exact boundary points are not represented by finite z; the optimization incumbent is therefore a separate feasible archive that retains and can return boundary points. The bounded-to-unconstrained conversion, including its numerical clipping, is performed only when a chain is initialized. Every subsequent transition threads the exact accepted unconstrained state; reconstructing and clipping it from the bounded coordinate would change the Markov state and invalidate the kernel.

Optimization versus sampling¶

Metropolis acceptance means that a phase-space proposal is valid for the fixed target under the documented assumptions. It does not mean the objective improved. An accepted transition may move uphill, while a rejected proposal may still contain a better feasible point worth archiving for optimization. NeuralOpt records transition acceptance, improvement over the exploration state, and best-incumbent updates separately.

The learned metric is fixed during one transition but may change between transitions with optimizer history. This is an adaptive optimization heuristic; no stationary-distribution or detailed-balance claim is made for that adaptive chain. The fixed identity-metric kernel is the reference sampling transition.

Executable example¶

import jax
import jax.numpy as jnp
from neuralopt import Problem, init_model, minimize
from neuralopt.hamiltonian import HamiltonianConfig

problem = Problem(lambda x: jnp.sum((x - 0.3) ** 2), (-2.0, 2.0), 4)
result = minimize(
    problem,
    params=init_model(jax.random.PRNGKey(0)),
    candidate_method="hamiltonian",
    hamiltonian_metric="identity",
    hamiltonian_config=HamiltonianConfig(step_size=0.03, leapfrog_steps=2),
    max_evaluations=100,
    record_diagnostics=True,
)
