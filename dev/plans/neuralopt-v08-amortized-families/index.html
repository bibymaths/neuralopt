NeuralOpt v0.8 plan: amortized optimization over problem families¶

Status: approved direction (Option A, 2026-11-22); this revision awaits approval. Nothing in it has been built. The claims it tests are listed, with their decision criteria, in docs/research/v0.8-claims.md.

Release freeze. - There is no v0.8.0 tag, PyPI upload or GitHub release until every claim in the register has a pre-registered result and the user has approved the release. - Until a claim's status is "supported", the README, docs, CHANGELOG and release notes call it "under test".

1. Goal¶

Many problems come as a family f(θ; c): - θ is the parameter vector to estimate, for example kinetic rate constants; - c is the instance's context: its observed data and its conditions, such as dose.

Members of a family share structure, and their solutions move with c.

Question. Can a model trained on one family supply starting points from which a local optimizer lands in the right basin more often and more cheaply than generic strategies? The generic strategies are a default start, multistart, and a nearest-neighbour lookup. And does any advantage survive distribution shift and pay back its offline cost?

What is learned and what is not: - Learned: the start predictor g_φ(c). - Not learned: the refinement, which is NeuralOpt's own classical, pure-JAX L-BFGS. This differs from v0.4–v0.7, where the learned part was the step rule and every learned geometry lost to a fixed diagonal or the BB step. - Kept: the learned-step transformer stays in the package as a documented research mode. Those negative results remain authoritative for it.

Prior expectation, stated honestly: - A nearest-neighbour start is often strong for smooth families. - Multistart may already be cheap for small, well-identified problems. - Gate F0 measures this before anything is learned.

2. Pipeline at inference¶

c ──► g_φ(c) ──► θ̂ (or K candidates + confidence) ──► owned L-BFGS ──► θ̂*
                                                          │
             low confidence or poor refined fit ──► fallback: owned multistart L-BFGS
  • Every objective and gradient call goes through one counted budget, including fallback calls.
  • The g_φ forward pass is not an objective evaluation. Its cold and warm time is measured and reported (claim C10).
  • No SciPy, Optimistix or other external solver runs at inference.

3. Component 1: owned L-BFGS (classical, not learned)¶

Purpose: the refinement for every NeuralOpt arm, and the local solver inside NeuralOpt multistart and fallback. It is documented as a classical method.

Design (neuralopt.lbfgs, opt-in, pure JAX)¶

  • Memory and direction: limited memory m (default 10). The two-loop recursion runs only on curvature pairs with sᵀy > ε‖s‖‖y‖; other pairs are skipped and the skip is counted.
  • Initial scaling γ = sᵀy / yᵀy from the newest pair.
  • Box bounds, by projection:
  • The free set is the variables not at a bound whose gradient would not push them out of the box.
  • The direction is the L-BFGS direction restricted to the free set; bound variables take the projected-gradient component.
  • Trial points are P_box(x + α d).
  • A backtracking Armijo condition is checked along the projection arc, with sufficient decrease measured against gᵀ(P_box(x + α d) − x).
  • The direction is reset to the projected gradient when it is not a descent direction.
  • Stopping:
  • the projected-gradient norm ‖P_box(x − g) − x‖_∞ falls below a tolerance;
  • the relative change in f falls below a tolerance;
  • or the budget is exhausted.
  • Accounting (the same convention as the NeuralOpt loop):
  • each line-search trial costs one objective call;
  • the gradient is computed only at the accepted point (one gradient call);
  • a trial is proposed only while used + 2 ≤ budget;
  • nonfinite values reject the trial and are counted;
  • counters are returned per solve.
  • Execution:
  • one lax.while_loop inside one jit;
  • vmap over a batch of (c, x0), and over K starts for multistart;
  • under vmap, both lax.cond branches are computed, but calls are charged only when taken, as in neuralopt.compiled.
  • Precision: float64 for all v0.8 work.

Tests (written first)¶

  1. Quadratics:
  2. Unconstrained SPD quadratics, d ∈ {2, 8, 32}, κ ∈ {1, 1e2, 1e4}: converge to the analytic minimizer, with projected-gradient norm ≤ 1e-8 within the budget.
  3. On a quadratic, the stored pairs satisfy y = H s to 1e-10.
  4. SciPy reference at matched budget, on the v0.7 engine-benchmark set (32 rotated κ = 100 quadratics, d = 16, 100 units each, results/engine-benchmark-v0.7.0.json protocol) and on the v0.7 rotated curvature tasks:
  5. the owned L-BFGS reaches regret ≤ 1e-6 on at least as many tasks as SciPy L-BFGS-B minus one;
  6. its median log10 regret is within 1.0 decade of SciPy's;
  7. its median units to 1e-6 are within 1.5× SciPy's.
  8. SciPy's counts use the existing harness accounting: every SciPy call is one objective plus one gradient.
  9. Bounded problems:
  10. quadratics whose minimizer lies outside the box, on a face, an edge or a corner;
  11. Rosenbrock in a box that clips the optimum.
  12. Each must reach the projected KKT point: projected-gradient norm ≤ 1e-8, with active bounds identified correctly. Iterates never leave the box.
  13. Accounting:
  14. counted objective and gradient calls equal wrapped-callback counts;
  15. the budget is never exceeded;
  16. nonfinite trials are rejected and counted.
  17. Parity:
  18. float64 single versus batched (vmap) solves give identical trajectories and counts to 1e-12;
  19. repeated solves with a new context of the same shape do not recompile.

4. Component 2: start predictor g_φ (the learned part)¶

  • Input: the context as a set of observation tokens. Each token holds (time, measured value, species/channel id, condition features such as dose, observation-noise level if known).
  • Instances may have different numbers and spacings of time points.
  • Masked self-attention runs over the tokens, then masked mean and attention pooling.
  • Condition variables shared by the instance enter as an extra token.
  • Output:
  • θ̂ in log-parameter space, mapped into the box with a scaled tanh (always inside the bounds);
  • option K: K candidate starts plus a confidence score: a calibrated probability that refinement from the top candidate reaches success.
  • Training data:
  • simulated instances: parameters sampled from the family prior, model simulated, realistic noise added;
  • solved instances: the reference optimum θ* of each training instance, from a harness-only reference multistart. Training uses no test instance.
  • Losses compared (pre-registered; the comparison is claim C7):
  • regression to the reference optimum: ‖ĝ − log θ*‖² in normalized coordinates;
  • objective-based: minimize f(θ̂; c), optionally after k unrolled L-BFGS steps;
  • for K starts, a best-of-K loss min_k ℓ(θ̂_k), with a diversity check so the candidates do not collapse.
  • The confidence head is trained with a binary loss on "refinement from this start reached success", from training-split solves only.
  • Inference:
  • one forward pass;
  • L-BFGS from the top candidate, or from all K candidates batched;
  • fallback to owned multistart L-BFGS when the confidence is below a threshold τ_c or the refined fit fails a fit check.
  • The fit check is the refined objective above a family-specific threshold derived from the noise model, for example a reduced-χ² bound.
  • τ_c and the fit threshold are tuned on validation only. The fallback calls count in the same budget.
  • Checkpoints: a versioned schema, neuralopt-start/v0.8-v1, that fails closed. Earlier checkpoint formats are unaffected.

5. Arms¶

All arms use the same instances, matched objective + gradient units, float64 and CPU. Wall time is reported cold, warm and batched.

# Arm Start Refinement Role
A0 default start + owned L-BFGS family default θ_d owned L-BFGS no amortization (C1 comparator)
A1 default start + SciPy L-BFGS-B θ_d SciPy harness reference
A2 multistart SciPy L-BFGS-B Sobol starts, count and split validation-tuned SciPy generic reference (C2 with A2o, C5)
A2o multistart owned L-BFGS K Sobol/random starts owned L-BFGS C2 uses the better of A2 and A2o on each axis; K-random comparator (C6); also the fallback
A3 nearest-neighbour start + owned L-BFGS θ* of the nearest training context owned L-BFGS lookup bar (C3)
A4 learned start + owned L-BFGS g_φ(c) owned L-BFGS primary (C1–C4, C9, C10)
A4K learned K starts + owned L-BFGS (batched) top-K of g_φ owned L-BFGS C6
A4F A4 with confidence check and fallback g_φ(c) owned L-BFGS + fallback C5, C8
A5 learned start + SciPy L-BFGS-B g_φ(c) SciPy harness only: separates the start from the refinement

Nearest neighbour: - Distance is measured on a context representation fixed before any test data is seen: - observations interpolated to a common time grid per species; - each time point standardized with training-split statistics; - condition features appended. - Lookup time is reported. Lookups use no objective units.

6. Metrics¶

  • Reference optimum f*: harness-only multistart L-BFGS-B with 64 Sobol starts × 2,000 units, plus the true-parameter start when it is known.
  • It must be reproduced by a second Sobol seed within ε on ≥ 99% of validation instances. If not, the budget is raised.
  • If any arm beats it, it is updated and that fact is reported.
  • Success: f(θ̂) − f* ≤ ε_abs + ε_rel·|f*|, with ε fixed per family before F0. Success is measured on the objective.
  • Parameter error: reported only together with an identifiability check: profile flatness, or the condition number and eigenvalues of the Fisher information at θ* (for least squares, JᵀJ/σ²).
  • Directions with flat profiles or tiny Fisher eigenvalues are reported as non-identified, not as errors.
  • Cost:
  • objective, gradient and HVP calls, always reported separately for every arm, because a JAX/ODE gradient costs several objective calls;
  • the measured gradient/objective cost ratio per family, from the F0 timing probe;
  • accepted steps;
  • wall time cold (including compilation), warm and batched;
  • g_φ and nearest-neighbour time, listed separately;
  • fallback rate and fallback units.
  • Time and units to success: censored at the budget. Also the success rate as a function of budget.
  • Break-even (C4): N* = T_offline / (t_generic − t_amortized).
  • T_offline = simulating the training set + solving all training targets (reference multistart) + training (compilation and updates).
  • t = mean time to success at a matched success rate.
  • Reported as a number with an interval and the measurement protocol. "No break-even" when t_amortized ≥ t_generic.
  • Statistics:
  • paired per-instance differences, positive = worse;
  • percentile-bootstrap 95% intervals over instances;
  • paired intervals for success-rate differences;
  • per-family and per-split tables with failure and nonfinite counts;
  • ≥ 5 predictor training seeds for learned arms, with each instance's outcome the mean over seeds; per-seed spread is reported;
  • every claim's criterion is applied per family/variant.

7. Splits (fixed, hashed, written once)¶

  • Instance i of split s uses seed = hash(family, variant, split, i).
  • Each split has a manifest listing contexts and true parameters, with its sha256 recorded.
  • No tuning, model selection or threshold is set on a test split.
Split Purpose Size (initial)
train simulated + solved instances for g_φ, the nearest-neighbour index, confidence labels 2,000
validation all tuning (baselines, τ_c, fit thresholds, early stopping) and F0 200
test (in-distribution) final comparison, opened once 200
test (OOD) final comparison, opened once 200 per shift

OOD shifts, each a separate named split: 1. a higher noise level; 2. true parameters outside the training range, but inside the optimization box; 3. different sampling times: count, spacing and sparsity; 4. Family 2 only: doses outside the training range.

8. Families¶

Family 1: damped-sinusoid fitting (synthetic, known multimodality)¶

  • Model: y(t) = a · exp(−λt) · sin(2πωt + φ) + ε, with θ = (a, λ, ω, φ), d = 4.
  • Context: the observed series.
  • Objective: the mean squared residual.
  • Why: frequency aliasing and harmonics give local minima in ω, whose number grows with the sampling span. This multimodality is known and controllable, and the truth is known.

Family 2: two-step Michaelis–Menten cascade (ODE, d = 4–5)¶

  • Model: S →(E1) I →(E2) P, with θ = log(V1, K1, V2, K2), plus an optional degradation rate (d = 5).
  • Solver: float64 fixed-step RK4 in JAX. The step is chosen so the error is ≤ 1e-8 relative against a 16× finer reference over the validation box.
  • Objective: weighted least squares over the observed species, with known noise σ.
  • Variants:
  • 2a, clean: dense sampling and low noise. This is the control.
  • 2b, realistic:
    • sparse sampling times (5–8 points, irregular);
    • noise at 5–10% of the signal;
    • condition-dependent inputs: dose (initial S0, or a bolus into S at a stated time), varied per instance;
    • only P observed, plus I at a subset of the times.
  • Variant 2b is intended to produce real local minima and near-flat directions. F0 checks whether it actually does, by counting the distinct converged optima per instance across reference starts.

Family 3: real dataset from the user's kinetic modelling work (later)¶

Only after Families 1 and 2 pass their gates, and under its own addendum: provenance, the split by experiment or condition, noise model, and licence.

9. Gate F0: headroom, with no learning¶

What runs: - Arms A0, A1, A2, A2o and A3 and the reference; - on the validation split of each family and variant; - at budgets of {25, 50, 100, 200, 400, 800} units.

Headroom on two axes, per family and variant: - B95_multi: the smallest budget at which multistart L-BFGS-B (A2) reaches 95% success. The owned version, A2o, is reported alongside. - B95_NN: the same for the nearest-neighbour start + owned L-BFGS (A3). - c_local: the median units of a single local solve from the default start (A0) to its own stopping criterion.

Stop rule (pre-registered before F0 runs): - A family or variant stops only if B95_multi ≤ 2 · c_local. Then multistart is essentially as cheap as one local solve, and there is no headroom to amortize. - If B95_NN ≤ 2 · c_local, a lookup already amortizes the family. This is recorded, and claim C3 becomes the decisive test for that family. - Also reported: the share of instances that no generic arm solves at 800 units (the hard set), and the number of distinct converged optima per instance.

Timing probe first: - It measures warm, batched µs per objective and per gradient call for each family. - The full F0 compute is projected from it. - Cap: 6 CPU-hours through F0. If the projection exceeds it, I stop and report before running.

10. Training targets and offline cost¶

  • Evaluation reference: 128k units per instance.
  • Training targets: a cheaper reference, 16 starts × 1,000 units. It must match the evaluation reference within ε on ≥ 98% of validation instances; if not, its budget is raised.
  • All of this cost is part of T_offline (C4).

11. Gates and stop points¶

Stop After Content
1 this plan, CLAUDE.md update and claims register committed (all claims "unverified") approval

3 the v0.8 pre-registration, frozen and hashed, with every claim's criterion approval before any training
4 the study (training, in-distribution test, OOD test) claims register updated; every outcome reported as found; release decision is the user's

Track v0.8-B (§15) starts only after stop point 2. It has its own plan approval and pre-registration stops before any implementation or training.

Exploratory results may be reported but cannot change a claim's status. Each stop is preceded by the full local quality gate.

12. Rules¶

  • Test first; float64; no uncounted evaluations; never form secants from un-evaluated states.
  • Frozen artifacts are never overwritten. v0.8 results are versioned (results/*-v0.8.0.json).
  • External solvers are references only. No PINNs or PDE collocation. Certification stays independent and model-free.
  • Squash merges with conventional PR titles. Research PRs may be merged only when every CI check passes. No tags or releases.

  • Sambharya, R., Hall, G., Amos, B., Stellato, B. Learning to Warm-Start Fixed-Point Optimization Algorithms. Journal of Machine Learning Research 25(166):1–46, 2024. arXiv:2309.07835.

  • Zammit-Mangion, A., Sainsbury-Dale, M., Huser, R. Neural Methods for Amortized Inference. Annual Review of Statistics and Its Application, 2025. doi:10.1146/annurev-statistics-112723-034123.
  • A survey of amortized parameter estimation with neural networks: the comparison point for g_φ as an amortized point estimator.
  • Chosen over Cranmer, Brehmer and Louppe (PNAS 2020), which is broader.

15. Parallel track v0.8-B: a learned step policy in its plausible regime¶

Detailed plan: docs/plans/neuralopt-v08b-step-policy.md (approved 2026-11-22; implementation waits for stop point 2 and the user's go-ahead). It fixes the metric definitions, decision rules, cost matching, compute cap and dependencies; where it is more specific than this section, it governs.

Status: recorded here; no implementation until the user approves its own plan. It has its own pre-registration, the same release freeze, and the same families, splits and baselines as F0. It starts only after F0 (stop point 2), so that it uses the verified families and baselines.

Question. v0.4–v0.7 tested learned steps on precision at moderate budgets and lost to fixed or BB steps. v0.8-B asks whether a learned step policy can win where such policies are most plausible: - multimodal and valley-shaped families: Rastrigin, Ackley, shifted Rosenbrock, Family 1 and Family 2b; - small budgets of 20–100 evaluations; - judged on anytime best-so-far progress and reaching the global basin, not on final precision.

Metrics: - anytime regret, the area under the best-so-far regret curve (claim C11); - the probability of reaching the global basin, measured by a measurement-only local polish (claim C12); - objective and gradient calls reported separately; - warm and cold wall time.

Baselines, matched on units and, separately, on warm wall-clock, each tuned on validation only: - owned multistart L-BFGS; - SciPy multistart L-BFGS-B; - CMA-ES; - TPE; - BB.

CMA-ES and TPE are harness-only references, from packages added to the benchmark extra. Gradient-free arms receive their whole unit budget as objective calls. The gradient-to-objective cost ratio is reported, so unit matching does not hide the price of gradients.

Policy (design space for its plan): - a transformer over the trajectory that outputs per-coordinate step sizes, as in POP (Kobiolka et al. 2026). POP runs a per-coordinate trajectory history through a transformer and samples coordinate-wise log step sizes from a Gaussian. - Optionally, Optimus-style low-rank updates (Gärtner et al. 2023). - Stochastic outputs for exploration, as in OPEN (Goldie et al. 2024). - It may read the instance context, as the start predictor does. - Checkpoints use a new versioned schema. The existing history-v1 and Gram schemas are untouched.

Training: - RL (for example PPO) or evolution strategies, on a clipped best-so-far improvement reward max(0, f_best(t−1) − f(x_t)), the form POP uses; - on the target families' training splits only, with the OOD splits held out; - no second-order unrolls. - A compute cap is set in its plan. POP reports about 56 H100-hours of training, which is far beyond this project's CPU budget. That scale gap is a stated risk to C11 and C12.

Claims: C11 (anytime regret) and C12 (global-basin probability) are in the register as unverified.
