NeuralOpt v0.8-B: a learned step policy in its plausible regime (plan)¶

Status: approved on 2026-11-22, with the decisions in §14. Implementation still waits for the main track's gate F0 (stop point 2) to be reported and for the user's go-ahead, so v0.8-B builds on the verified families, owned L-BFGS and baselines. No training or heavy compute happens before then.

This plan expands section 15 of neuralopt-v08-amortized-families.md. It keeps the v0.8 release freeze (CLAUDE.md rule 8), the same Families 1 and 2b with their hashed splits, the same accounting conventions (§6 of the main plan), and has its own pre-registration. Claims C11 and C12 are in docs/research/v0.8-claims.md as unverified; their criteria below are proposed and are frozen only at this track's pre-registration.

1. Question and hypotheses¶

v0.4–v0.7 tested learned steps on final precision at moderate budgets; fixed diagonal or BB steps won. v0.8-B asks whether a learned step policy wins where such policies are most plausible: multimodal or valley-shaped problems, at 20–100 evaluations, judged on anytime progress and on reaching the global basin, not on final precision.

  • H11 (C11): on a family, the policy's anytime regret (§5.1) is lower than every baseline's at every pre-registered budget, under unit matching and under wall-clock matching.
  • H12 (C12): on a family, the policy reaches the global basin (§5.2) with a higher probability than every baseline, at every pre-registered budget, under both matchings.

Both are falsifiable per family: either can come out supported, not supported or inconclusive (§6), and every outcome is reported.

2. Families and splits¶

All families are f(x; c) on a box, float64, d = 4 in distribution.

  • Splits: train 2,000, validation 200, test 200, and 200 per OOD shift. Instance i of split s is seeded by sha256(family|split|i), as in neuralopt.families; every split has a sha256 manifest. Families 1 and 2b reuse their existing splits and hashes.

  • First evaluation point. Every arm's first evaluation on instance i is the same point x0_i: drawn uniformly in the box (instance-seeded) for the synthetic families, and the family default for Families 1 and 2b (as F0's A0). Multistart arms take their further starts from a fixed Sobol sequence.

3. Arms¶

3.1 The policy (the learned arm)¶

  • Inputs, all in box-normalized coordinates u = 2(x − lo)/(hi − lo) − 1, with gradients mapped as g_u = Jᵀ g_x, J = diag((hi − lo)/2):
  • per coordinate and per step, over a window of the last H = 16 evaluated states: u_j, g_u,j divided by the window's RMS gradient, and the secant differences Δu_j, Δg_j, masked when invalid or rejected (never filled with zeros);
  • per step: the normalized objective relative to the best so far, the step index and the remaining budget fraction;
  • Families 1 and 2b only: an instance-context embedding from the start predictor's masked set encoder. The synthetic families get no context, because their only context (shift and rotation) would reveal the optimum.
  • Architecture: POP-style (Kobiolka et al. 2026). A small transformer attends over time per coordinate (weights shared across coordinates), followed by one cross-coordinate mixing layer so that rotated problems can be represented. Target size 20k–50k parameters.
  • Outputs: per-coordinate mean and log standard deviation of a log step size, η_t ~ N(μ_t, σ_t²), sampled for exploration (as in OPEN; Goldie et al. 2024). The update is u_{t+1} = Π_box(u_t − exp(η_t) ⊙ g̃_t).
  • Optional ablation: an Optimus-style low-rank term (rank 2) added to the update (Gärtner et al. 2023). It is off in the primary arm.
  • Evaluation randomness: the sampling RNG is seeded per instance, so evaluation is reproducible.
  • Cost: one objective and one gradient call at each new point (2 units).
  • Checkpoints use a new versioned schema (step-policy-v1); the history-v1 and Gram schemas and all v0.1–v0.7 checkpoints are untouched.
  • Every step at inference comes from NeuralOpt's own code (CLAUDE.md).

Stochastic baselines (CMA-ES, TPE, quasi-random search with its scramble seed) are run with 5 seeds; each instance's outcome is the mean over seeds, as for the policy.

4. Cost accounting and matching¶

  • Units. One objective call = 1 unit; one gradient call = 1 unit; HVPs are not used. Objective and gradient calls are reported separately for every arm.
  • Owned L-BFGS: an objective per line-search trial and a gradient on acceptance (§3 of the main plan).
  • SciPy, BB and the policy: a joint value-and-gradient call costs 2 units. Its value becomes available at the second unit.
  • CMA-ES, TPE and random search use no gradients. At budget B they make B objective calls, and their gradient count is zero.
  • Unit matching undercharges gradients. F0's probe measured the gradient at 2.5× an objective call for Family 1 and about 12× for Family 2. Unit matching therefore favours gradient-based arms. This is why every claim must also hold under wall-clock matching. A cost-weighted count n_f + r·n_g, with the measured ratio r, is reported as an exploratory column.
  • Wall-clock matching (warm).
  • Each arm's median warm cumulative-time curve C_a(t) (seconds after t units) is measured per family on 50 validation instances. Measurement is one instance at a time (the deployment case), after compilation, on the same CPU, float64, with XLA single-threaded. The policy's forward passes are included. The curves are frozen in the pre-registration.
  • The wall budget for budget B is T(B) = C_policy(B). Baseline k gets B_k = max{t : C_k(t) ≤ T(B)} units and is tuned on validation at B_k.
  • Wall-matched anytime regret integrates L (§5.1) over time [0, T(B)], mapping each arm's units to time through its frozen C_a. This keeps the comparison deterministic.
  • Actual test timings are recorded too. A deviation from the frozen curves above 25% is reported.
  • Cold time (including compilation) and batched throughput are reported separately and are not part of the criteria.
  • Not charged to any arm: the global-basin polish (§5.2) and the regret normalization scale s_i (§5.1). Both are measurement only and never feed back into an arm.
  • Charged as offline cost and reported: training (all generations, including compilation) and the training-split references.

5. Metric definitions¶

5.1 Anytime regret: the area under the regret curve¶

For instance i:

  • f_best(t) is the lowest objective value among the arm's objective evaluations completed within the first t units. It is undefined before the first value.
  • Floor: τ_i = ε_abs + ε_rel·|f*_i|, the success tolerance, with ε as in F0 (ε_abs = 1e-6, ε_rel = 1e-3). Precision below the success tolerance earns nothing, so the metric does not reward final precision.
  • Normalization: s_i is the median of f(x) − f*_i over 64 fixed Sobol points in the box (harness only, uncharged). It is the regret of a typical uninformed point. Instances with s_i ≤ τ_i are excluded and counted.
  • Clipped log regret: L_i(t) = log10( min(max(f_best(t) − f*_i, τ_i), 10·s_i) / s_i ), and L_i(t) = 1 before the first value.
  • Its range is [log10(τ_i/s_i), 1].
  • If any arm finds a value below f*_i − τ_i, then f*_i is updated for all arms and the update is reported, as in F0.
  • Anytime regret at budget B: AUC_i(B) = (1/B) Σ_{t=1..B} L_i(t). Lower is better. The wall-matched version is (1/T) ∫_0^T L_i(τ) dτ, as in §4.
  • Budget grid: B ∈ {20, 50, 100} units.
  • Baselines are run separately at each B, with their B-tuned configuration.
  • The policy takes the remaining budget as an input and is also run separately at each B.
  • Full curves for t = 1..100 are reported for every arm.

5.2 Global basin¶

  • Definition (operational): an arm reached the global basin at budget B if a measurement-only polish started from its best point x_best(B) converges to within the success tolerance of the global optimum: f(polish(x_best)) − f*_i ≤ τ_i.
  • The polish is NeuralOpt's owned L-BFGS, capped at 2,000 units with gtol 1e-8 (the reference cap), with no restarts. It is uncharged and never fed back.
  • The global basin is thus the basin of attraction of a fixed local polish, not a geometric object.
  • Per family:
  • Rastrigin, Ackley, shifted Rosenbrock: f* = 0 at x = o (known).
  • Families 1 and 2b: f* is the reference optimum from the main plan's §6 protocol (SciPy multistart, 64 Sobol starts plus the truth, two seeds, ≥ 99% two-seed reproduction on validation), updated if any arm beats it.
  • Sanity rows (reported): the rate from polishing x0_i directly (zero search), and the rate from polishing the best of B Sobol points.

6. How C11 and C12 are decided¶

  • Unit of analysis: test instances, paired across arms. The policy's outcome for an instance is the mean over ≥ 5 training seeds. Stochastic baselines are averaged over 5 run seeds.
  • Differences:
  • C11: d_i = AUC_i(policy) − AUC_i(baseline); negative is better for the policy.
  • C12: d_i = p_i(policy) − p_i(baseline), where p_i is the share of seeds reaching the global basin; positive is better for the policy.
  • Intervals: a two-level percentile bootstrap resamples training seeds, then instances (paired). It uses 10,000 resamples and a fixed RNG seed.
  • Each comparison uses a two-sided 95% interval.
  • "Beats all baselines" is an intersection-union test (Berger 1982), decided per family. The claim holds for a family only if every comparison is individually significant: 6 baselines × 3 budgets × 2 matchings = 36 comparisons.
  • Because the claim is a conjunction, its error rate is at most the level of each comparison: a one-sided 2.5% chance of a false "supported" for the family.
  • No multiplicity correction is applied within a family, and none to the per-family statuses in the register.
  • Joint claims across families need a multiplicity correction. Any statement made jointly about several families must apply Holm–Bonferroni over the families it covers, at a family-wise one-sided level of 2.5%. Examples: "C11 holds on the multimodal families", "on k of 5 families", or a pooled summary.
  • Each family's IUT p-value is the largest one-sided bootstrap p-value among its 36 comparisons.
  • A family counts toward the joint statement only if it passes at its Holm-adjusted level.
  • Without that correction, the per-family statuses are reported side by side and no joint claim is made.
  • C11 status per family:
  • Supported: for every baseline, budget and matching, the 95% interval of d lies entirely below 0.
  • Not supported: for some baseline, budget and matching, the interval lies entirely above 0 (that baseline is better).
  • Inconclusive: otherwise.
  • C12 status per family: the same rules with the sign reversed. Supported requires every lower bound > 0; not supported requires some upper bound < 0. Degenerate cases (all rates 0 or all 1) are inconclusive.
  • Each family's status is recorded separately. OOD results are reported (C8 conventions) and do not change the status.
  • Power (meaningful effects approved: 0.25 decades of AUC; 10 percentage points of basin rate):
  • Rule: each comparison is powered at 80% for the threshold effect at the 95% two-sided level. Required paired instances n = ((z_0.975 + z_0.8)·SD_d / δ)², with z_0.975 + z_0.8 = 2.80.
  • Under the IUT, the claim's power is at most the weakest comparison's, so n is set by the comparison with the largest SD_d. That is usually the closest baseline.

  • A priori (normal approximation, assumed SDs; no data yet). At n = 200 the minimal detectable effect is 0.198·SD_d. The 99% rule of the first draft needed 0.242·SD_d.

    Effect δ SD_d n needed (95%) n needed (99%, superseded) MDE at n = 200 (95%)

7. Training¶

  • Method: antithetic evolution strategies (Salimans et al. 2017) with centred-rank fitness shaping and Adam (optax, already in the train extra) on the ES gradient estimate.
  • ES needs no backpropagation through unrolls, so there are no second-order unrolls. It also accepts the non-differentiable, clipped metric directly.
  • PPO is a pre-declared fallback (decision 2), triggered only by the validation rule below.
  • Reward: clipped best-so-far regret at every unit.
  • Per unit, r_t = −L_i(t) (§5.1), so an episode's mean reward is −AUC_i(B), the evaluation metric itself.
  • POP's per-step form max(0, f_best(t−1) − f(x_t)) telescopes to the total improvement and ignores when progress happens. The per-unit clipped regret keeps the anytime character and is clipped at the same floor and cap.
  • Data: each target family's training split only.
  • Synthetic f* = 0 is known.
  • Families 1 and 2b use the training-target references of the main plan (§10: 16 starts × 1,000 units, owned).
  • Validation is used only for selection (best checkpoint every 25 generations) and early stopping. Test and OOD splits stay closed until the single final evaluation.
  • Settings:
  • population P = 64 (32 antithetic pairs), ES noise 0.02;
  • M = 16 training instances per generation, common to the population;
  • budget sampled per episode from {20, 50, 100};
  • G ≤ 500 generations;
  • one policy per family; 5 seeds.

7.1 Pre-registered PPO fallback (validation only)¶

  • Validation checkpoints: every 25 generations, each ES seed's current policy is evaluated on the family's validation split. The tracked score is the mean AUC(B) over B ∈ {20, 50, 100} under unit matching.
  • Plateau: a seed has plateaued when either
  • at least 200 generations have run, and its best validation score improved by less than 0.02 decades over the last 4 checkpoints (100 generations); or
  • it reaches G = 500.

A family's ES training has plateaued when at least 3 of its 5 seeds have. - "Beats quasi-random search and BB on validation": the seed-mean policy (best validation checkpoint per seed) has a lower mean validation AUC(B) than both quasi-random search and BB at every B ∈ {20, 50, 100}, under unit and wall-clock matching. These are point estimates on validation; no intervals are used for this switch. - Trigger: PPO runs for a family if its ES training has plateaued and the ES policy does not beat quasi-random search and BB on validation. The trigger is evaluated once, when the family's ES runs stop. - PPO arm (pre-declared now): - the same policy network as actor, plus a value head; - clipped surrogate 0.2, GAE λ = 0.95, γ = 1 (finite horizon); - the same per-unit reward −L_i(t), the same training split and validation selection; - 5 seeds; - no new dependencies (JAX and optax). - It differentiates through the policy network per step, never through the optimization trajectory, so there are no second-order unrolls. - Which arm goes to test: exactly one policy arm per family. It is ES if PPO was not triggered, PPO if it was, fixed before any test data is opened. The other arm's validation results are reported. Test data never informs this choice. - Compute: PPO counts against the same 24 CPU-hour cap. If the projection exceeds the cap, PPO's update count is cut first; seeds are never cut.

8. Compute estimate and cap¶

The per-unit costs below come from F0's probe (results/f0-probe-v0.8.0.json). The policy forward cost is an estimate that the B0 probe must measure.

  • Per generation: 1,024 episodes × up to 50 policy steps = 51,200 steps.
  • Synthetic families and Family 1:
  • about 26 µs per step (about 20 µs policy forward plus 2 cheap evaluations);
  • ≈ 1.3 s per generation, ≈ 11 min per run, plus validation checks;
  • 4 families × 5 seeds ≈ 4 CPU-hours.
  • Family 2b:
  • about 220 µs per step (≈ 100 µs per unit for batched owned evaluation, × 2, plus the forward pass);
  • ≈ 11 s per generation, ≈ 1.6 h per run;
  • 5 seeds ≈ 8 CPU-hours.
  • Baseline tuning, references for the new synthetic splits, wall-clock calibration and the single test evaluation: ≈ 4 CPU-hours. Synthetic references are free (f* known). Family 2b references dominate.
  • Total ≈ 16 CPU-hours. Proposed cap: 24 CPU-hours.
  • If the B0 probe projects more than the cap, the pre-registered reduction order is: G 500 → 300, then M 16 → 8, then dropping the d = 8 OOD split.
  • Seeds (≥ 5) and test sizes are never reduced.
  • For scale, POP reports about 56 H100-hours of training. A CPU budget three orders of magnitude smaller is a stated risk to C11 and C12; a negative result under this budget is reported as such and not generalized to learned policies at scale.

9. Long runs¶

Every run expected to exceed 10 minutes follows CLAUDE.md rule 9: - a named tmux session (for example neuralopt-v08b-train); - a git-ignored runs/v08b-<stage>-<UTC stamp>/ directory holding: - command.txt: command, git SHA, uv.lock hash, UTC start; - log.txt; - an atomic progress.json: units (family × seed) done/total, generation, ETA; - checkpoints: one per finished unit via scripts/run_checkpoint.py, plus ES state every 25 generations, so a resume loses at most 25 generations; - exit_code, and DONE only on success. - HANDOFF.md is updated before and after each run. - Versioned results go to results/v0.8-b-*.json. Frozen files are never overwritten.

14. Decisions (approved 2026-11-22)¶

  1. Quasi-random search is in the "every baseline" set (§3.2).
  2. ES first, PPO deferred, with a pre-registered PPO fallback triggered on validation only (§7.1).
  3. A 24 CPU-hour cap with the stated reduction order (§8).
  4. optuna for TPE, in the benchmark extra only (§10).
  5. Power thresholds: 0.25 decades of AUC; 10 percentage points of basin rate (§6).
  6. Change: C11 and C12 are decided per family as intersection-union tests with two-sided 95% intervals per comparison. A multiplicity correction (Holm–Bonferroni over families) applies only to claims made jointly across families. The power check was redone under this rule (§6).
