Gibbs measures and optimization¶

At temperature T, the artificial finite-dimensional Gibbs density is pi_T(x) proportional to exp(-f(x)/T). It is a distribution over feasible states, not an optimizer certificate. Lower temperature concentrates mass near low objective values under suitable assumptions but does not guarantee that a finite trajectory finds a global minimizer.

The logistic bounded transform includes its determinant so fixed-geometry continuous-time dynamics target the mapped bounded Gibbs measure. Euler, BAOAB, and adaptive learned geometry introduce distinct qualifications:

  • Euler–Maruyama and BAOAB have finite-step bias.
  • Episode-frozen learned geometry is fixed only during one episode.
  • Updating geometry from history makes the overall process nonstationary.
  • Changing temperature creates a sequence of targets, not one invariant Gibbs distribution.

Optimization therefore reports the best feasible incumbent separately from the stochastic state. Distributional accuracy, candidate improvement, and incumbent updates are independent measurements.
