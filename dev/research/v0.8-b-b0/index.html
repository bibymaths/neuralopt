v0.8-B step B-S2: baselines and headroom, with no learning¶

Status: step B-S2 of docs/plans/neuralopt-v08b-step-policy.md (§11). Nothing here is learned. Claims C11 and C12 stay unverified. B-S2 decides, per family, whether there is room for a learned step policy to win, using the plan's pre-specified B0 rule.

  • Artifact: results/v0.8-b-b0.json, combined from runs/v08b-b0-20261122T2029Z/.
  • Code: scripts/run_b0_v0_8b.py, scripts/v08b_baselines.py, the synthetic families in neuralopt.families; branch research/v0.8-b-s2, commit b634a50.
  • Run: one process per family, pinned to one core; every thread count set to 1.
  • Data: the validation split only, 200 instances per family. Test and OOD splits were not opened.

Deviations and caveats¶

  1. The BB tuning grid is smaller than the plan's. The plan listed an initial step of {1e-3, 1e-2, 1e-1} and a step clip of {1, 10}. NeuralOpt's compiled BB loop (the Gram loop with zero parameters, which v0.7 checked equals BB to 1e-10) has no step-clip parameter. So only the fallback (initial) length was tuned.
  2. Owned L-BFGS traces: rejected line-search trials count in the units, but the solver does not expose their values. Such a trial could be lower than the current value only by the tiny Armijo margin (c1 = 1e-4), so the arm's anytime regret is very slightly pessimistic.
  3. CMA-ES evaluates the shared start point first (1 unit), then runs its generations. The last generation is cut at the budget and not told to the optimizer.
  4. The B0 rule's "best baseline" is the maximum over all arms and configurations on the same 200 instances. That is optimistic for baselines, so it makes "no headroom" easier to declare.
  5. For Rosenbrock and Family 2b, the conclusion does not depend on it: several arms exceed 0.95, and the zero-search polish alone reaches 0.88 and 0.98.
  6. The basin metric saturates where the polish does the work. The uncharged 2,000-unit polish defines the basin operationally (plan §5.2). On Family 2b and Rosenbrock, almost every start lies in the global basin, so C12 cannot separate arms there. The B0 rule then also drops them from C11, as the plan specifies. Their anytime regret still differs between arms (owned multistart −3.9 versus BB −2.9 on Family 2b), but that is not tested.
  7. Rastrigin and Ackley may give little signal on C12. Basin rates of 0–17% for every baseline mean a policy must find the global basin among thousands of local minima within 100 evaluations. A negative or inconclusive C12 there is a plausible outcome.
